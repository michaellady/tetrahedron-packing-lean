# Regular tetrahedron packing — prior art and Lean feasibility

Research date: 23 November 2026. I found no later work beating **4000/4671** for unconstrained packings of congruent regular tetrahedra in three-dimensional Euclidean space, or determining the unrestricted optimum. I independently replayed the record construction and the one-particle lattice benchmark with exact rational arithmetic. I found no end-to-end Lean certificate for the record in the indexed repositories and snapshots searched.

[Integer arithmetic](TetrahedronPacking/RecordArithmetic.lean) checks 1,996 ordered pairs and shifts in the uniform box `[-2,2]³` using ten integer normals. [Shift bounds](TetrahedronPacking/PeriodicSeparation.lean) and [real geometry](TetrahedronPacking/RecordGeometry.lean) lift those comparisons to pairwise disjoint interiors of the entire infinite orbit. The geometry also checks squared Euclidean edge lengths and the Lebesgue volume of the primitive cell. The coordinates use a uniform scale of 160 relative to the paper. [Measure lemmas](TetrahedronPacking/PackingMeasure.lean) prove volume additivity for interior-disjoint convex pieces, bounded-window inclusions for counting them, and the asymptotic lattice point count in coordinate cubes.

The [simplex volume theorem](TetrahedronPacking/SimplexVolume.lean) partitions the unit cube by coordinate order and transports volume by an affine map. It gives the Lebesgue volume of each tetrahedron. The [periodic density theorem](TetrahedronPacking/PeriodicDensity.lean) squeezes actual covered volume in expanding cubes between the whole-piece counts in two cubes of fixed margin. The resulting limit is exactly $4000/4671$ for origin-centered coordinate cubes of all real radii tending to infinity. The coordinates are multiplied by 160, which rescales the cube radii and multiplies both volumes by the same factor. An independent reviewer reconstructed the published coordinates, tested the uniform shift box and reviewed the density statement and its hypotheses. Theorem 1's space-group and dimer-transitivity assertions are outside the density certificate's scope.

Pair-specific bounds improve on a global shift box even when the global cutoff shrinks in a supercell. The [exact Python benchmark](benchmark_exact.py) replayed 32- and 256-tetrahedron supercells in 2,464 and 19,712 separation checks. The corresponding uniform boxes would require 27,616 and 1,769,216 checks. These supercells repeat the record and keep the same density. The [generic Lean bridge](TetrahedronPacking/IntegerCertificate.lean) proves that checked integer projections in an inclusive shift box suffice given independently verified vertex bounds and agreement of the integer displacements with the real period chart. The record proof uses the same bridge with a uniform box. [Python benchmark output](benchmark_exact.txt)

The [full-cell checker](TetrahedronPacking/CellChecker.lean) uses integer vertices and period columns. It checks both identities for a supplied inverse numerator and verifies its denominator is positive. For each pair, exact integer projections of the vertices determine the candidate shift intervals by ceiling and floor. The checker enumerates those shifts and computes the translated offsets itself. It excludes only an identical motif index at shift zero. The [density theorem](TetrahedronPacking/CellDensity.lean) takes accepted pair checks through real disjoint interiors and the volume limit, and the regularity check verifies the common squared length and a nonzero edge determinant.

The [strict-improvement theorem](TetrahedronPacking/CellImprovement.lean) takes a finite integer-coordinate witness. It certifies common regular shape, disjoint interiors of all translates, and a spatial density strictly above $4000/4671$ if the integer cross-product inequality holds. Rational vertex and period data fit by clearing a common denominator. Each cell must supply a full-rank lattice, a valid inverse numerator and separating axes that pass the checker. The axis verifier proves soundness of the supplied directions. An actual improvement still requires finding new cell data.

The [restricted determinant proof](TetrahedronPacking/RestrictedDeterminant.lean) expands the determinant from the parameterized periods, proves the sharp scalar ratio bound from the stated constraints, and checks attainment at the published positive parameters. It covers the algebraic portion of Lemma 4. The geometric feasibility proof for the entire family is a separate obligation. [CEG equations (6), (7), (11) and (13)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0586v3)

An [exact restricted-family probe](restricted_probe.py) bounds the shift box uniformly over the parameter tetrahedron. Each Cramer numerator is a determinant of three columns affine in the parameters. At a convex combination of parameter corners, multilinearity expresses the numerator as a weighted average of determinants with columns drawn from independently chosen corners. The script bounds all these mixed determinants and divides by the positive determinant lower bound $42039/1000$. The largest resulting coordinate interval width is $1280/519<3$, so a possible overlap forces each integer shift into $[-2,2]$.

Of the 1,996 cases in that box, 16 lacked a shared signed normal across all four parameter corners. They all used shifts $(0,-1,1)$ or $(0,1,-1)$, corresponding to $\pm(a-b)$. Splitting at $u=0$ gave a shared normal for every case on each side. Vertex coordinates and lattice shifts for fixed integer coefficients are affine in the parameters, so such a shared normal extends to each convex half. The corners used by the script are exactly the vertices of equation (7): with $s=u/2+2v$, the four normalized slacks proportional to $33/320+w\pm s$ and $3/64-w\pm v$ are nonnegative and sum to one after division by $3/10$. The split corners add the four edge intersections with $u=0$. This is an exact computation plus a mathematical coverage argument. The interpolation and mixed-corner bounds still need Lean proofs. ## The question and the best established bounds

The packing part of Hilbert's **Problem 18** explicitly asks how to pack equal solids as densely as possible and includes “regular tetrahedra with given edges”. Lagarias and Zong, *Mysteries in Packing*, state the modern question verbatim as **Problem 2**: “What is the value of δᶜ(T)? Can the optimum be achieved by a periodic packing?” Here $T$ is a regular tetrahedron and the superscript $c$ denotes congruent copies. The strict record-beating target $δ^c(T)>4000/4671$ is one possible outcome. Equality would require an upper bound for the entire class. [Hilbert, 1902 English translation, p. 467](https://people.reed.edu/~davidp/341/resources/hilbert.pdf) · [Lagarias–Zong, p. 1547](https://www.ams.org/notices/201211/rtx121101540p.pdf)

The lower bound is Chen–Engel–Glotzer’s **Theorem 1** in both the journal and arXiv v3. The journal locator is §2.4 and the preprint locator is §II.D. Both give the coordinates in equations (15)–(16). The simulation table is Table 1 in the journal and Table I in v3. **Lemma 3** proves feasibility over the restricted parameter set $P''$. **Lemma 4** proves the maximum over that set. This is a specific three-parameter family with prescribed incidences. The paper explicitly leaves broader optimality open. [Journal PDF](https://glotzerlab.engin.umich.edu/home/publications-pdfs/2010/Fs00454-010-9273-0.pdf) · [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0586v3)

The rigorous global upper bound I found is Gravel–Elser–Kallus **Theorem 5.2**, $1-2.6\ldots\times10^{-25}$. It finds disjoint incompletely covered balls near tetrahedron edges and recursively lower-bounds the missing volume in each. The gap is roughly 0.144 in volume fraction. Fodor and Grundbacher explicitly still report these endpoints in a 2026 paper. Torquato–Jiao’s narrower-looking numerical upper estimates in §III use an unproved local-to-global premise. [Gravel–Elser–Kallus](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-010-9304-x) · [2026 cross-check](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2026.115087) · [Torquato–Jiao §III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0011)

## What the searches already tried

- **Monte Carlo compression.** Chen–Engel–Glotzer §3 explored $N=1,\ldots,16$ using random displacements, rotations and cell shear. Their $N=4,8,12,16$ record-like outcomes repeat the same four-particle structure. Table I reports success rates of 80%, 44%, 3% and below 1% respectively. The $N=15$ numerical run even falls below a known construction formed by removing a particle from a supercell. This is direct evidence that a failure to improve via compression can reflect trapping. I did not reproduce those stochastic rates. [CEG arXiv v3, §III and Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0586v3)
- **Adaptive shrinking cell.** Torquato–Jiao vary the cell strain along with particle positions and rotations. Their later §IV reports numerical experiments with 2–32 particles per cell. The densest outcomes found in those experiments had a four-particle basis. That does not exhaust cells of those sizes. [ASC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.0940) · [Later experiments, §IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0011)
- **Periodic divide and concur.** Kallus–Elser–Gravel use a second numerical method that alternates local pair exclusions with global agreement across periodic replicas. Their paper reports rediscovering the record in 15 of 100 starts within the iteration limit, with an illustrative run in Fig. 4. I located the algorithm description and numerical examples. I did not locate or replay the original implementation. [Divide and concur, §V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.3301)
- **Available simulation software.** HOOMD-blue HPMC implements hard convex-polyhedron simulation and provides a compression updater. This is usable for candidate generation. The 2026 confined-tetrahedron study also gives a concrete example of pressure-driven HOOMD compression with up to 20 particles in a primitive cell. That experiment has cylindrical boundaries. [HPMC methods paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.04692) · [HOOMD compression example](https://hoomd-blue.readthedocs.io/en/v5.4.0/tutorial/00-Introducing-HOOMD-blue/05-Compressing-the-System.html) · [Confined search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26627)
- **Unrestricted upper-bound routes.** A convex-body extension of Cohn–Elkies uses functions on the Euclidean motion group and semidefinite optimization. This gives a method to investigate. I found no certified improvement for the regular tetrahedron from it. ProofAtlas reports some narrower bounds around the double-dimer construction and counterexamples to proposed reductions involving raw Voronoi cell volumes and stacking constraints. I treat those entries as leads. I did not validate their underlying restricted proofs. [Congruent-body upper-bound framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4893) · [ProofAtlas collection](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/densest-regular-tetrahedron-packing/)

A positive construction already has a physical precedent. The 2023 gold nanotetrahedron study observed the predicted dimer packing and quasicrystals under different particle-shape, ligand and assembly conditions. A 2025 experiment produced a different, deliberately low-density octo-diamond crystal with a 64-particle cell. A denser ideal packing supplies coordinates for a new target structure. Assembly and stability remain empirical questions. [Gold dimer experiment](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.3c05299) · [Octo-diamond experiment](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41563-025-02185-y)

## Exact replay and certificate design

Run `python3 replay.py` from the workspace root. [Script](replay.py) · [Observed output](replay.txt)

The density expression immediately after equation (12) in arXiv v3 prints a factor of two with $U=9$ for one tetrahedron. The specified primitive cell contains four tetrahedra. The factor of four gives the stated theorem value and agrees with Appendix D, C3+opt, which lists $V=42039/1000$. The replay and Lean model use the four pieces explicitly. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0586v3)

1. **Encode the right translation lattice.** CEG's (a,b,c) are the parity coordinates in Definition 2. The four-particle motif has translation basis $B=(a+b,b+c,c+a)$. The second dimer is the inversion of the first translated by $d+a$. The script computes squared edge length **18**, tetrahedron volume **9**, cell volume **42039/1000**, and density **4000/4671**. The independent one-particle input from arXiv v3 Appendix D1 gives **18/49**.
2. **Prove shift coverage.** For each ordered pair of tetrahedra, bound each coordinate of $B^{-1}x$ by the minimum and maximum at its four vertices. If the two closed hulls can meet across shift $Bk$ with $k\in\mathbb Z^3$, each integer $k_j$ lies between the difference of the first minimum and second maximum and the difference of the first maximum and second minimum. The script enumerates those intervals after rounding inward to integer endpoints. For the original basis, it checked **308** ordered candidates, with largest coefficient magnitude **2**. For the unimodular basis change $B_1\mapsto B_1+3B_2$, it checked **932**, with magnitude **7**. These are bounding-box candidate counts. The paper's separate 26-neighbor reduction is proved in the parity coordinates $(a,b,c)$ and in its restricted family.
3. **Allow contact and reject overlap.** The checker skips only the same motif index at shift zero. It checks a nonzero linear functional whose maximum on one vertex set is at most its minimum on the other. Convexity gives the weak inequalities on the hulls. Openness at an interior point makes the inequalities strict there, giving disjoint interiors. The replay accepted the face-sharing dimers. It rejected an inserted duplicate at shift zero and another planted across index shift $(0,-3,0)$. Thus a universal `[-1,1]^3` assumption in arbitrary basis coordinates is unsound.
4. **Connect arithmetic density to geometric density.** The Lean proof computes the covolume of the translation lattice and the Lebesgue volume of each tetrahedron. It counts whole tetrahedra in inner and outer windows to bound pieces that cross the observation window. The two bounds have the same limit, which gives the determinant ratio as the spatial density. [Record specialization](TetrahedronPacking/RecordDensity.lean)

Strict improvement has a useful finite-witness structure. For bounded congruent bodies under the upper box-density convention, periodic densities approximate the unrestricted supremum by taking a large high-density box, keeping its wholly contained particles and repeating it. The loss from the boundary strip tends to zero. This explains why periodic cells are meaningful on the construction side even though attainment by a single periodic packing is a separate question. For any strictly better periodic cell, a small uniform shrink about each tetrahedron's centroid preserves a strict density advantage while creating clearance. Rational rigid motions can approximate the poses, using the rational rotation matrix of a nonzero rational quaternion, and rational vectors can approximate the lattice basis. These are proof sketches for future work. I have not formalized the reduction or obtained a bound on the period or the rational encoding size. [Periodic-density framework](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/finite-packing-and-covering/congruent-convex-bodies/B1A13A94CD3B86DF611698A36A148721)

As an additional computational check, I reran the CEG case with planes through triples from the combined eight vertices. That second axis-generation rule accepted all 308 candidates too. It shared the same coordinates, shift bounds and rational arithmetic. It checks the axis search by a different construction and provides no independent check of coverage.

## Most useful next work

1. **Validate new candidate data with the shared checker.** A rational candidate needs scaled integer vertices and period columns, a checked inverse, a finite list of successful directions, and a strict cross-product comparison. The checker derives the pair bounds and offsets from these data. Larger motifs also make the all-pairs enumeration cost significant.
2. **Run a genuinely different search for a higher-density witness.** Broad small-cell compression is prior art. One narrower direction is the finite-replacement route recorded by ProofAtlas. Remove every reference tetrahedron in interior conflict with a proposed finite inserted patch. If the patch contains more tetrahedra than it removes, spacing copies of the replacement far apart in a large supercell would give a periodic density increase. The risk is finding a positive-count patch among highly constrained continuous poses. A bounded failure would describe that search family. [Finite-replacement route](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/densest-regular-tetrahedron-packing/)
3. **Consider a restricted upper bound with a stated continuous feasible set.** CEG already optimize $P''$. An extension needs to cover an explicitly larger family, such as cells outside the contact equalities or a broader class than opposite face-sharing dimers. This requires certified continuous coverage and all relevant lattice shifts. Local minima and numerical failure supply no such coverage.

A record-beating exact cell is the milestone that would supply a new candidate structure to materials scientists. The current deliverables establish reproducible benchmarks and identify the proof components still needed. They give no new packing bound.
